## 1. The Current Affairs Reality: Drop the Daily Paper Guilt for Prelims

### The Truth
- Aspirants spend 90 to 120 minutes every morning dissecting The Hindu.
- In UPSC Prelims, current affairs accounts for **12–16 questions**.
- Out of those, over **70% are linked to international organizations, treaties, places in news, and key government schemes** — which are easily covered in monthly consolidations!
- Daily newspaper reading produces severe **diminishing returns** for Prelims. (It is crucial for Mains analysis and essay, but our immediate battle is clearing Prelims).

### The Working Professional Solution
- **Zero newspaper reading in the morning.** Reclaim those 90 minutes for static core (Polity, Economy, Geography, Environment).
- **The Lean Current Affairs Stack**:
  1. One curated monthly compilation (e.g. Vision IAS / UnlockIAS / PT 365).
  2. Weekly 30-minute theme reviews (International Bodies, Treaties, Conflict Zones, Protected Areas).
  3. Static linkage: When an economic or political term appears in the news, revise the underlying NCERT / Laxmikanth concept.

## 2. The OMR Answering Rule: Neutralizing Overthinking

### The Mechanism of Your Failure in Past Attempts
In previous attempts, you scored 40, 28, 55, and 62. You identified that you often knew the right answer initially, but second-guessed and changed it to a wrong answer.

### Neil Sir's Antidote: "Mark Then and There in the OMR"
1. **No "Floating" Question Marks**: Do not circle an option in the booklet with the plan to "re-think it later."
2. **First Instinct Rule**: When you read a question and your intuition/knowledge points to an option, that instinct is powered by subconscious memory.
3. **The Trap of the Second Pass**: When you return to a question in the last 20 minutes, your brain is fatigued, panicked about marks, and begins manufacturing artificial logic. It talks you out of the correct answer.
4. **The Execution**:
   - Evaluate the question.
   - Apply elimination.
   - If confident or narrowed to a 2-option split: **Bubble the OMR immediately.**
   - Once bubbled, it is physically impossible to change. The question is closed. Your mind moves forward.

## 3. The Golden Attempt Band: 90 to 95 Questions

### The Arithmetic of Prelims
- **GS Paper I**: 100 questions, 2 marks each, –0.66 negative marking.
- If you attempt **65–70 questions** (fearful approach):
  - 45 correct = 90 marks, 25 wrong = –16.6 marks → **Net = 73.4 marks (FAIL)**.
  - To clear with 70 attempts, you need an impossible 85%+ accuracy.
- If you attempt **100 questions** (reckless approach):
  - You force in 8–10 pure wild guesses that leak negative marks.
- **The Golden Band (Neil Sir)**: **90 to 95 questions**.
  - Covers everything you know directly.
  - Covers all 2-option eliminated questions (+0.67 expected value per question).
  - Leaves only 5 to 8 questions that are completely alien.

## 4. CSAT Without a Maths Background: The Sherlocking Method

### The Qualifying Reality
- 80 questions | 200 marks | 2 hours.
- Qualifying bar: **33% = 66 marks** (approx 27 net correct questions).
- It does **not** count toward your merit rank. You only need to pass.

### Neil Sir's Blueprint for Non-Maths Students:
1. **Comprehension is the Core Lever**:
   - There are ~25 to 27 Reading Comprehension questions in every CSAT paper.
   - They require **zero mathematics formulas**.
   - With structured practice ("Sherlocking" — finding explicit textual evidence, eliminating extreme words), you can achieve 20–22 correct out of 25.
   - 20 correct × 2.5 = **50 marks** from comprehension alone!
2. **Easy Quant & Logical Reasoning**:
   - Syllogisms, Directions, Blood Relations, Linear/Circular Seating, Basic Percentages, Calendar, Clocks.
   - These provide another 15–18 accessible questions.
   - Solving 12–15 of these correctly adds **30–37.5 marks**.
   - Total: **50 + 32 = 82+ marks**, comfortably sailing past the 66 qualifying bar.
3. **The Skip List**:
   - Complex Permutation & Combination.
   - Convoluted Probability puzzles.
   - Multi-variable Diophantine equations.
   - *Rule*: If a math problem takes more than 90 seconds to visualize, **SKIP IT IMMEDIATELY**.
