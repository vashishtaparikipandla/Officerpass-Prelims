# Current Affairs Engine — Architecture

> **Problem**: Newspapers have 100 articles/day. UPSC asks 15-20 current affairs questions.  
> **Solution**: Filter ruthlessly. Study only what UPSC will ask.

---

## The UPSC Current Affairs Filter

Not everything in the news is UPSC-worthy. Apply this filter:

### HIGH UPSC PROBABILITY (Always Track)
- Government schemes (new launches, modifications, expansions)
- International treaties, agreements, summits India participates in
- Supreme Court landmark judgments
- Constitutional amendments and new laws
- Economic policy changes (RBI, Budget, trade)
- Environmental: COP meetings, endangered species, new protected areas
- Science & Tech: ISRO missions, defense tech, new diseases
- Awards: Nobel, Bharat Ratna, international awards
- India's foreign policy moves (bilateral, multilateral)
- Census data, economic survey highlights

### MEDIUM PROBABILITY (Scan Weekly)
- Bilateral relations (India + specific country)
- UN reports mentioning India
- Major infrastructure projects
- Sports events India hosts or wins

### LOW PROBABILITY (Ignore)
- Political party dynamics, elections, rally coverage
- Crime news, accidents
- Celebrity news
- State-level routine governance
- Opinion pieces, editorials (unless on constitutional matters)

## How To Read Current Affairs Daily (15-20 minutes)

### Option A: Curated Sources (Recommended for 15 min/day)
1. **PIB (Press Information Bureau)** — Government's own releases (5 min scan)
2. **The Hindu Editorial Summary** (Insights IAS or similar) — 5 min
3. **Monthly compilation** (Vision IAS, Drishti, or Vajiram) — Weekend read

### Option B: If You Read The Hindu Daily
- Read ONLY: Front page, Editorial page, National, International, Science
- Skip: Sports, Entertainment, Business (unless policy-related)
- Time limit: 20 minutes max. Use a timer.

### Option C: I Build It For You
You upload relevant articles or tell me topics in the news, and I'll create
UPSC-filtered notes with potential questions. This is the most efficient approach
given your 3-4 hour constraint.

## Current Affairs × Static Integration

The real UPSC hack: Connect current affairs to static syllabus.

**Example**:
- News: "India signs Free Trade Agreement with EU"
- Static Link: WTO rules on FTAs, MFN clause, Article XXIV of GATT
- UPSC Question: Tests your knowledge of BOTH the news AND the static concept

**Method**: For every important current affairs item, ask:
1. What is the STATIC concept behind this news?
2. What from Polity/Economy/Geography connects to this?
3. How would UPSC frame a question combining both?

This is how we build the "UPSC thinking machine" you asked for.
